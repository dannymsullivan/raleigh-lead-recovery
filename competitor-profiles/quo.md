## Summary

Quo is a broad business phone system, formerly OpenPhone. It sells a shared phone and inbox platform for calls, texts, team collaboration, call routing, CRM integrations, and its Sona AI agent.

Raleigh Lead Recovery should not try to look bigger than Quo. The better wedge is narrower:

> We are not replacing your phone system. We catch the plumbing calls you already miss, set it up for you, and show you the jobs that would have gone to voicemail.

That wedge is stronger for local plumbers because it removes three objections:

- "I do not want to change my phone system."
- "I do not have time to configure AI."
- "I do not know whether missed calls are actually costing me."

## What Quo Does Well

### 1. Clear enemy

Quo repeatedly frames missed calls as lost opportunity. Its home-services page makes the pain concrete for field teams: calls happen while someone is on a roof, under a sink, or done for the day.

Use this, but make it more local and direct:

> If voicemail answers, the next Raleigh plumber has a shot at the job.

### 2. Multiple proof types

Quo uses review volume, customer logos, case studies, videos, and specific outcome stats. We do not have that yet, so we should use proof we can actually support:

- Demo number.
- Sample report.
- Founder setup.
- First real pilot results.
- Before/after missed-call count.
- Actual captured call examples with masked details.

### 3. "Set up in minutes" friction reduction

Quo pushes easy setup and free trial. Our stronger version is:

> You do not set it up alone. Danny helps configure missed-call forwarding and reviews the first week.

For plumbers, done-for-you beats self-serve.

### 4. Industry page

Quo has a home-services page, but it is still broad. It mentions roofs, sinks, staff, property owners, tasks, CRMs, and team collaboration.

Our opportunity:

> Raleigh plumbers only. Plumbing intake only. Missed calls only.

### 5. AI details without sounding too technical

Quo explains that Sona can answer, collect details, summarize, transfer, and log calls. We should explain Dana the same way, but with plumber-specific examples:

- Leak location.
- Drain backup.
- Water heater issue.
- Sewer smell or backup.
- No hot water.
- Emergency vs. routine callback.

## What Not To Copy

- Do not position as a full business phone system.
- Do not compete on per-user pricing.
- Do not promise "set up in minutes" if the offer is actually white-glove.
- Do not lead with integrations before the missed-call audit proves value.
- Do not claim 24/7 support or appointment booking until the pilot flow reliably handles it.
- Do not overstate revenue. Use "pipeline value" and "captured job details."

- Quo is broad. Raleigh Lead Recovery speaks directly to plumbing calls.
- Quo starts with a platform. Raleigh Lead Recovery starts with proof.
- Quo pricing looks cheap per seat, but the buyer still has to configure, test, and operate it.

## Pricing Implication

Do not list public pricing yet. Quo's sticker price is low because it is SaaS per seat. Raleigh Lead Recovery is a done-for-you local service with setup, tuning, and reporting.

Recommended first pricing path:

1. Keep the public offer as a free 7-day audit.
2. For the first 3 pilot customers, quote a founding local plan after the report:
   - Solo/owner-operated shop: $399/month.
   - Small crew with higher call volume: $599-$799/month.
3. Include setup and weekly report review.
4. Add usage guardrails if AI call volume gets high.
5. Raise price once there are 2-3 real examples of recovered jobs.

How to explain the price:

> Quo is a phone system you configure. Raleigh Lead Recovery is a local missed-call recovery service I set up, monitor, and tune for your plumbing calls.

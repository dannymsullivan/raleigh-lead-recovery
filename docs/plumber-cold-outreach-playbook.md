# Raleigh Lead Recovery Plumber Outreach Playbook

Use this for a small, personal outbound push to Raleigh-area plumbing shops. The goal is not to blast a list. The goal is to find owners who already know missed calls cost them jobs and offer a low-friction 7-day audit.

## Target List

Start with 25-50 shops across Raleigh, Cary, Apex, Garner, Wake Forest, Durham, and Chapel Hill.

Prioritize shops that show at least one of these signals:

- Advertise emergency plumbing or after-hours service.
- Run Google Ads for plumbing keywords.
- Have reviews mentioning slow callbacks, voicemail, or hard-to-reach dispatch.
- Are owner-operated or small enough that the owner still answers calls.
- Have a mobile-friendly site but no obvious after-hours intake workflow.

Skip for now:

- Large franchise branches with corporate call centers.
- Companies that already emphasize 24/7 live answering.
- Shops with no visible phone number or no clear local footprint.

Starter list:

- See `docs/plumber-prospect-list.csv` for a first batch of Raleigh/Triangle plumbing prospects with fit notes and next actions.

## Prospecting Tools

Start manual for the first 25-50 shops. Use Google Maps, the company website, Google reviews, and a spreadsheet. You are not trying to build a perfect database yet. You are trying to learn which local plumbers feel the pain enough to reply.

Useful workflow:

1. Search Google Maps for `plumber Raleigh`, `emergency plumber Raleigh`, `water heater repair Raleigh`, `drain cleaning Raleigh`, plus Cary, Apex, Garner, Wake Forest, Durham, and Chapel Hill.
2. Open each company website and check whether they mention emergency service, after-hours service, family owned, owner operated, or a real person answering.
3. Skim recent Google reviews for clues like missed calls, voicemail, fast callbacks, emergency jobs, after-hours help, or owner involvement.
4. Add only the best-fit shops to the sheet. Do not scrape 500 contacts before you know what works.

Tool options once the manual workflow is working:

- Outscraper: good for exporting Google Maps-style local business lists to CSV, Excel, or JSON. Use it when you want to expand from 50 researched shops to a few hundred local businesses.
- Clay: good for enrichment, lead scoring, and custom research workflows. Use it later if you want to score businesses by website signals, ads, reviews, and contact data in one table.
- Apollo: good for finding B2B contacts and running email sequences. It may be less complete for owner-operated local plumbers than for larger B2B companies, so verify every contact before emailing.
- Instantly: good for cold email sending and deliverability once you have a clean list and a proven message. Do not start here before the offer is validated manually.

My recommendation: for the next week, use a CSV or Google Sheet only. Send highly personal emails, make manual calls, and track who replies. Add paid tooling only after the first 25-50 touches show a pattern.

## Fit Score

Score each shop before outreach. Contact A prospects first.

Priority A:

- Locally owned or clearly owner-led.
- Emergency, water-heater, drain, sewer, or leak repair is prominent.
- The business looks busy, but not so large that a corporate call center probably handles everything.
- Reviews mention urgent jobs, fast response, callbacks, or after-hours help.
- The website gives you a clear phone number and service area.

Priority B:

- Good service fit, but the company may already have mature dispatch or after-hours coverage.
- Multi-trade companies where plumbing is one major line of business.
- Larger review volume or multiple locations, but still locally relevant.

Priority C:

- Large franchise, private-equity-style operator, or corporate branch.
- Already markets 24/7 live answering heavily.
- Contact path is only a generic corporate form.
- Good later expansion target, but not the best first customer.

## Core Positioning

One-line pitch:

> I help Raleigh plumbers catch missed calls before they turn into the next plumber's job.

Offer:

> Free 7-day missed-call audit. No new customer-facing number, no card, no software change.

What you are asking for:

> Permission to test call forwarding for missed, busy, or after-hours calls and show them what would have gone to voicemail.

## Outreach Cadence

Day 1: Cold email that asks them to call the demo number.

Day 2: Manual call to the shop line.

Day 2: Voicemail if no answer.

Day 4: Follow-up email with the demo number.

Day 7: Final close-the-loop email.

Do 10 shops per day for the first week. Keep notes on current missed-call handling:

- Voicemail
- Owner cell
- Answering service
- Office manager
- Unknown

That answer tells you whether they are a fit.

## Instagram And Facebook DMs

Use DMs as a warm, manual channel. Do not blast the same message to dozens of accounts. The goal is to start a real local conversation, not to look like an automation bot.

Best order:

1. Follow the business account or owner account.
2. Like or comment on one relevant recent post if there is a natural reason.
3. Send one short DM.
4. If they do not reply, follow up once a few days later.
5. Stop after that unless they engage.

Instagram is best when:

- The shop posts job photos, owner videos, van photos, or behind-the-scenes work.
- You can reference a specific service, neighborhood, or emergency job they posted.
- The owner appears active on the account.

Facebook is best when:

- The company has an active local business Page.
- The owner is visible in local business groups.
- You can ask for an intro through a mutual friend or local Raleigh connection.

Avoid:

- Sending identical messages at scale.
- Pitching in public comments.
- Dropping a link before they ask.
- Using automation tools for first-touch DMs.
- Messaging personal profiles in a way that feels like a scraped lead list.

## Manual Call Script

```text
Hey, this is Danny in Raleigh. I built a simple missed-call recovery service for local plumbers.

Quick reason I'm calling: if your line is busy, unanswered, or after-hours, Dana can answer, collect the plumbing issue and callback number, and text it to you.

I'm offering a free 7-day audit so you can see what calls would have gone to voicemail. Is the owner the right person to ask about that?
```

If they ask what it costs:

```text
The audit is free. After a week, we look at how many real calls were caught and whether it makes sense to keep running it. If it does, we can talk about a simple monthly plan.
```

If they ask whether customers call a different number:

```text
No. Customers keep calling your normal number. This only catches calls that are busy, unanswered, or after-hours.
```

If they ask whether it books jobs:

```text
Not by default. It captures the lead details and tells the caller a real plumber will call back. The goal is to stop good calls from disappearing into voicemail.
```

## Personalization Notes

Use one relevant detail. Do not overdo it.

Good:

```text
Saw you advertise emergency water heater service around Raleigh.
```

Good:

```text
Noticed your reviews mention quick service after hours, which usually means the phone matters a lot.
```

Weak:

```text
I came across your website and was impressed by your commitment to customer service.
```

## Compliance Notes

This is operational guidance, not legal advice.

For cold email:

- Use accurate sender and reply-to information.
- Do not use deceptive subject lines.
- Include a physical mailing address.
- Include a clear opt-out.
- Honor opt-outs promptly.

For phone:

- Make manual calls only.
- Avoid autodialers, prerecorded calls, AI voice calls, and bulk SMS.
- Do not cold text personal cell numbers without opt-in.
- Keep an internal do-not-contact list.
- If someone asks not to be contacted, stop.

Useful references:

- FTC CAN-SPAM business guide: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- National Do Not Call Registry FAQ: https://www.donotcall.gov/faq.html

1. Add 10 researched shops.
2. Send Email 1.
3. Call yesterday's 10 shops.
4. Leave voicemail only when useful.
5. Log the current missed-call handling if you learn it.
6. Send follow-ups only to shops that still look relevant.
